# Final Report — Over Delivery List-Buying Research

**Prepared:** August 1, 2026  
**Framework:** Brian Kurtz / *Over Delivery* direct-response discipline

## Executive conclusion

The best acquisition strategy is **not to buy a large email list and blast it**. The stronger model is to buy **controlled access to relevant, permissioned publisher audiences**, convert that traffic into first-party leads and customers, and compound the learning inside an owned audience.

The recommended sequence is:

1. **Newsletter and publisher sponsorships** for trusted, relevant audiences.
2. **Performance partnerships and affiliates** where revenue-share or CPA economics fit.
3. **First-party lead capture** on every campaign so rented reach becomes owned audience.
4. **Traditional list/data vendors** only for direct mail, modeling, enrichment, hygiene, or clearly compliant deployment.
5. **Small, measurable tests** before any scale decision.

A source is valuable because of its audience truth, congruence, testability, and relationship quality—not because it advertises the largest database.

## The key market distinction

| Category | What it really provides | Correct use |
|---|---|---|
| Publisher/list owner | Audience relationship and media inventory | Newsletter sponsorship, dedicated send, direct mail, native placement |
| Broker/list manager | Access, selection, clearance, merge/purge, scheduling | Build and clear a test universe |
| Newsletter marketplace | Access to publisher inventory | Compare placements and run controlled sponsorship tests |
| Solo-ad marketplace | A dedicated send from another party's audience | Small tracked tests only; demand consent and refund controls |
| Affiliate/performance network | Partner distribution and measurement | CPA, rev-share, creator, affiliate, and publisher recruitment |
| Data/list vendor | Records, modeled audiences, enrichment, hygiene, or deployment | Direct mail, modeling, append, compliant data use—not automatic email permission |
| Discovery database/association | Research and relationship infrastructure | Find current owners, managers, publishers, and media contacts |

**Critical boundary:** subscriber counts, network size, platform tracking, or “opt-in” sales language do not prove that addresses can be exported or used for unsolicited email.

## Recommended first testing lanes

### 1. Permissioned newsletter/publisher media

Start with relevant publisher sales teams and newsletter marketplaces such as Paved, beehiiv Ad Network, SmartBrief, Search Engine Journal, Morning Brew, and carefully selected vertical publishers. Buy the placement—not the subscriber file.

Require:

- audience source and paid/free mix;
- geography and recency;
- placement format and sender identity;
- dedicated versus native placement;
- click definition and bot filtering;
- reporting and make-good terms;
- complaint, unsubscribe, and suppression handling;
- written clearance for the offer and creative.

### 2. Performance partnerships

Use affiliate and partner systems when the economics support tracking, customer service, refunds, creative support, and revenue share. The specific partner and promotional method matter more than the network brand.

Do not assume that a network's ability to track email or affiliate traffic means every partner has permission to promote every offer by email. Obtain offer-level approval in writing.

### 3. Solo ads

Treat Udimi and similar marketplaces as **testing environments**, not as proof of list quality. Use one seller, one creative, one landing page, unique tracking, a fixed test budget, and written delivery/refund terms. Never scale on clicks alone.

### 4. Postal and data

Use NextMark and broker/manager conversations to discover named buyer/subscriber lists and direct-mail opportunities. Ask for RFM-style selects wherever available:

- **Recency:** last purchase or inquiry;
- **Frequency:** number of purchases or renewals;
- **Monetary value:** cumulative spend or value band.

Use data providers for direct mail, modeling, enrichment, hygiene, and compliant deployment—not as a shortcut to permissioned email media.

## Operating rules

1. Define the source, channel, sender, and product being purchased.
2. Separate buyer, paid subscriber, free registrant, lead, clicker, modeled record, and unknown.
3. Preserve source and consent evidence before launch.
4. Clear the offer and creative with the owner or publisher.
5. Predefine cost, attribution, contribution margin, complaint, refund, and pause thresholds.
6. Use unique URLs, promo codes, pixels, phone numbers, or campaign IDs.
7. Track leads, sales, refunds, chargebacks, complaints, unsubscribes, and downstream opt-ins.
8. Feed the results back into segmentation, creative, product, and house-file decisions.
9. Protect the publisher's relationship; do not sacrifice a valuable audience for a short-term test.
10. Treat rented reach as an acquisition and learning channel. The compounding asset is the owned audience.

## Compliance gate

Before any email or lead campaign, document the exact signup language, consent source, timestamp, channel, geography, frequency, advertiser disclosure, sender identity, and suppression process. Review CAN-SPAM and applicable state law; review GDPR/UK PECR where relevant; account for CCPA/CPRA sale/share opt-outs; and confirm that the selected ESP permits the proposed source type.

Operational guardrails from the research include keeping spam complaints below provider thresholds, honoring unsubscribes quickly, and defining hard-bounce, complaint, duplicate, invalid-lead, refund, pause, and make-good terms in the contract.

This report is an operating guide, not legal advice.
