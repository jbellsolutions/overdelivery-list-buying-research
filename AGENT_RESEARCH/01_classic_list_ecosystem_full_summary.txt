## Practical interpretation for the Over Delivery tradition

- **Best starting points for classic list work:** EMG, Conrad Direct, Geon, DirectMail.com, AmeriList, Global Direct, and Response Dynamics.
- **Best discovery/research layers:** NextMark and SRDS. They are not substitutes for a broker’s judgment; they expose datacards, list managers, owners, counts, rental bases, and policies.
- **Best owner-side management candidates:** EMG, Conrad Direct, Geon, Global Direct, and AmeriList.
- **Best postal/direct-response orientation:** Conrad Direct, EMG, Geon, DirectMail.com, AmeriList, and Response Dynamics.
- **Email requires extra scrutiny:** The traditional ecosystem distinguishes **opt-in email lists** from generic purchased-email databases. Global Direct explicitly says “opt-in email”; other vendors’ email offerings should be checked for permission, deployment method, suppression handling, and whether the buyer receives a rental deployment or a transferable file.
- **Pricing is rarely public:** The common pattern is quote-based pricing, with list-level base rental prices exposed through SRDS/NextMark or obtained from the manager/broker. No reliable universal minimum rental quantity was found in the reviewed official pages.
- **Excluded:** ListGiant-style mass consumer-data/lead-list sellers and cold-email database vendors were not included in the recommended ecosystem because they are materially different from permissioned, response-based, brokered list rental and management.

### Research limitations

- Several traditional firms publish no public pricing, minimums, file counts, or current rental policies.
- Some older firms retain live but dated websites; “active” here means the official site, contact details, and service pages were live during review—not that every listed file is currently rentable.
- The strongest next verification step for any solo buy or media buy is to request the current datacard, universe count, base/continuation rental price, minimum quantity, recency, source/permission description, NCOA/CASS or hygiene policy, suppression requirements, and whether the order is a one-time rental, continuation rental, exchange, or managed deployment.
