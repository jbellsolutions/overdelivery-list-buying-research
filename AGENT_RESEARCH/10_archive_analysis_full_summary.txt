## Source map: Brian Kurtz / *OverDeliver* archive

### 1. Core list-buying mechanics

**Primary source:**  
`/Users/home/Desktop/Top 1% Copywriting/01_OverDeliver_Book/transcripts_audio/session-03-transcript-pdf.txt`

- **SRDS as the historical list directory:** transcript pp. 19–20, lines 565–571  
  > “The first is what's called the Standard Rate and Data Directory of Business Lists… [a] huge, approximately 10-pound, six-inch thick directory… [with] approximately 35,000 listings and descriptions of mailing lists and customer lists and demographic lists you can rent…”

- **Build a prospect definition from behaviors and affinities:** pp. 19–20, lines 573–587  
  > “There are probably 25 different ways you can target those people and 25 different lists you can rent…”  
  Examples include automobile owners, tennis/golf subscribers or buyers, and geographic sub-categories.

- **Use list brokers to identify and organize candidate lists:** p. 20, lines 593–600  
  > “You can also get a hold of list brokers. These are men and women in companies around the country who specialize in accessing and organizing and acquiring… mailing lists or lists of names, addresses and phone numbers…”

- **Trade publications, newsletters, and associations as list sources:** p. 20, lines 602–607  
  > “Almost every business category… has a trade magazine or a trade newsletter or an association they are a member of… [and] all of those organizations make their list of members or subscribers available for rental or purchase.”

- **Research buying behavior before selecting a list:** pp. 21–22, lines 609–645  
  > “What other products or services go along with my product or service? What do they buy after they buy my product or service?”  
  Kurtz/Abraham then recommend identifying the companies serving those adjacent needs and contacting them directly.

- **Direct arrangements with list owners:** p. 22, lines 654–672  
  > “Call and contact the owners or the highest-level decision makers at those companies and work out some kind of arrangement where those companies share with you, for some kind of a share of the profits or fee, the names of their customers and their prospects.”

  Important detail: the passage explicitly includes **unconverted prospects** as potentially high-value names.

- **Use qualified lists beyond mailing:** pp. 23–24, lines 706–719  
  > “We got a list… of the names, the addresses, the phone numbers and the key decision makers… He was able to call ahead… and his commissions tripled.”  
  > “You can use the mailing list for calling ahead, for telemarketing, for calling after a mailing.”

- **Systematic repeated communication to a tightly defined list:** pp. 24–25, lines 737–756  
  Example: a workout attorney identified 1,200 financial institutions and mailed useful information monthly for two years. The transcript claims approximately $600/month produced more than $10 million in annual fee income.

### 5. Historical framing and live-validation flags

Treat the following as historical claims or archive-era context, not current market facts:

1. **1980s–1990s direct-mail scale:**  
   The “100 million pieces” claim and Michael Fishman’s role are in `035-direct-marketers-saving-lives.txt`, lines 61–65.

2. **SRDS directory size and format:**  
   “10-pound, six-inch thick,” “35,000 listings,” and “about 70 brokers” appear in the Jay Abraham transcript, `session-03-transcript-pdf.txt`, lines 565–571 and 637–645. Current SRDS products, availability, pricing, and broker directories must be checked live.

3. **Direct Mail Marketing Association references:**  
   The transcript names the “Direct Mail Marketing Association of America” and offices in New York/Washington, lines 642–645. Organization name, successor organization, and contact details require validation.

4. **Rental economics and pricing:**  
   The $100,000 one-time rental check in *The Moose on the Table*, lines 87–99, and “over $500 per thousand” in *The Politics of Direct Marketing*, line 87, are period-specific. Do not use them as current CPM/list-rental benchmarks.

5. **Case-study revenue claims:**  
   The workout-attorney example—$600/month producing more than $10 million annually—appears in `session-03-transcript-pdf.txt`, lines 737–756. It is an anecdotal historical case, not independently verified performance data.

6. **Boardroom company/list claims:**  
   “Best lists on the market,” millions of names, affluent executives, and “everyone used the Boardroom lists” in *The Moose on the Table*, lines 59–71, are Kurtz’s retrospective account and should not be generalized to current Boardroom list availability.

7. **Historical company valuations and subscriber counts:**  
   The persona file and other archive material mention multi-hundred-million-dollar Boardroom operations and large subscriber counts. These should be validated against primary corporate or contemporary reporting before publication.

8. **Titans event and offer history:**  
   The Titans event was in 2014, and the DVD/list-partnership offer in `043-the-politics-of-direct-marketing.txt`, lines 127–137, is archival. Any associated email address, product, list, or partnership opportunity should be treated as stale unless confirmed.

9. **Current email/list compliance:**  
   The archive predates or does not fully address current consent, privacy, CAN-SPAM, GDPR/UK GDPR, state privacy laws, suppression handling, deliverability standards, and platform-specific rules. Any modern implementation needs separate legal and operational validation.

## Bottom line

The archive’s central worldview is:

- **The list is the asset; names are not interchangeable.**
- **List selection and segmentation often matter more than additional copy polish.**
- **Buy or access lists through brokers, publishers, associations, adjacent product owners, and negotiated exchanges.**
- **Ask what prospects buy before, alongside, and after the offer.**
- **Treat list owners and customer relationships as ethical responsibilities, not merely inventory.**
- **Test lists, offers, and creative deliberately; direct-mail cost is a forcing function for discipline.**
- **For email, carry over the discipline and relationship logic even when marginal send cost is nearly zero.**
