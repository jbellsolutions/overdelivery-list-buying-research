# Buyer checklist: rented email lists, solo ads, newsletter sponsorships, affiliate email traffic, and direct mail

**Scope / caveat.** Practical compliance research for U.S., UK and EU/EEA campaigns. Not legal advice. Requirements depend on recipient status (consumer, sole trader, corporate subscriber), sender/advertiser roles, product sector, state/member-state implementation, and current regulator guidance. The EU ePrivacy Directive is implemented nationally; local rules can be stricter. The ICO flags some guidance as under review after the UK Data (Use and Access) Act.

## Executive rules

- **U.S. email:** CAN-SPAM is primarily an opt-out regime for commercial email, not a purchased-list permission system. Commercial email must use accurate headers/from lines, non-deceptive subject, identify ad/solicitation unless prior affirmative consent, include a valid physical postal address, and provide a functioning opt-out mechanism. Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days and for at least 30 days after sending; no fee, login, or information beyond email address (and preferences) may be required. The advertiser and any agency/sender can face exposure; outsourcing delivery does not transfer responsibility. State privacy/consumer-protection, sector rules, and platform policies can be stricter.
- **UK individual email/text:** PECR Reg. 22 generally requires **specific consent** or a narrow soft opt-in for individuals. Soft opt-in is only for the sender's own existing customers/prospective customers who bought or negotiated to buy a similar product/service, with an opt-out at collection and in every message; it does not cover bought-in lists or new prospects. Corporate bodies can generally receive unsolicited email, but sole traders and some partnerships are treated as individuals. Sender identity and a valid opt-out/contact address are required.
- **EU/EEA email:** ePrivacy Directive Art. 13 requires prior consent for electronic mail direct marketing, subject to the same-seller/similar-products exception. Member-state implementation may be stricter and may distinguish corporate subscribers. GDPR still governs the personal-data processing: lawful/fair/transparent, purpose-limited, minimised, accountable; document consent or legitimate-interest analysis; give notices (including indirect-source notices where applicable); honor direct-marketing objections immediately; use processor contracts and appropriate security.
- **UK/EU postal mail:** PECR does not regulate postal marketing. Postal campaigns still need a UK GDPR/GDPR lawful basis, transparency, data-source diligence, suppression/objection controls, retention limits, and any national do-not-mail/consumer rules. ICO lists postal marketing as “no [PECR] requirement,” likely legitimate interests or consent under data protection law.
- **Direct mail U.S.:** CAN-SPAM does not apply to physical mail. Truth-in-advertising, consumer-protection, sector-specific laws, state privacy law, do-not-contact/suppression requests, and data-broker/contract terms still matter. Avoid deceptive mail pieces, misleading sender identity, and unsupported claims.

## Channel-by-channel operational checklist

### 1) Rented/bought email lists

**Do not treat a list rental as a transfer of permission.**

Before launch require:
1. Written provenance: source URL/form, collection date, geography, audience type, exact consent language, whether consent named your brand or a defined category/third party, and whether email was specifically covered.
2. Contract allocates compliance, but retain independent verification/audit rights; require suppression propagation, complaint/bounce reporting, incident notice, data deletion/return, no re-use, and indemnity (indemnity does not eliminate regulator exposure).
3. Sample/audit consent records; reject vague “partners,” “selected companies,” pre-ticked boxes, silence, or consent for “offers” that does not identify email and sender/category. UK ICO says generic third-party consent is insufficient for bought-in email/text lists and consent must be specific, recent enough, accurate, fairly collected, and recorded.
4. Confirm data-role allocation under GDPR/UK GDPR: controller/joint controller/processor; if a broker processes for you, execute Art. 28 terms. If data came indirectly, plan Art. 14 transparency or document a valid exception; do not assume the broker's privacy notice covers your use.
5. Run the list through your global suppression list before upload; maintain a permanent “do not contact” suppression record. Do not delete an opt-out record in a way that allows re-import from a future rental.
6. Start with a controlled seed/test segment, monitor complaints, bounces, spam traps and unsubscribe rates, and pause on abnormal signals. Preserve evidence of what was sent, to whom, by which vendor, and when.

**Jurisdiction result:** A rented list that may be usable under U.S. CAN-SPAM can still be unlawful for UK/EU consumers without sender-specific consent. For cross-border buying, require the highest common standard: explicit, brand/category-specific email consent plus documented provenance.

### 2) Solo ads / publisher sends

A solo ad publisher sends your creative to its audience; you are typically the advertiser and may be an “instigator” even if the publisher presses send.

- UK ICO expressly says PECR applies to anyone who sends **or instigates** electronic-mail direct marketing; advertiser and sender can both be responsible. A written contract and compliance checks are recommended; a bulk-email platform is only technical delivery and does not normally become responsible.
- Require the publisher to identify the legal sender/advertiser, use a real reply/unsubscribe route, suppress prior opt-outs, and provide delivery evidence. Prohibit forwarding schemes that ask subscribers to send marketing to friends unless separately compliant; ICO says instigated viral marketing remains covered.
- In the U.S., ensure CAN-SPAM elements appear in the actual message and your organization is accurately identified; do not let a publisher use misleading headers or subject lines.
- For UK/EU consumer audiences, do not buy a publisher's claim that “our subscribers opted in” unless the evidence shows consent to receive **your** message/from you or a specifically identified category. “Newsletter subscribers” ≠ consent to unrelated advertisers.

### 3) Newsletter sponsorships / advertorials

- Distinguish a paid ad placement from editorial content. UK CAP Code rules 2.1–2.4 require marketing communications to be obviously identifiable; unsolicited email marketing must be identifiable without opening; advertorials must be clearly labeled (e.g., “Advertisement feature”). CAP 3.1/3.3 prohibit material misleadingness and omission of material information.
- Put a clear label in the subject/preheader and near the sponsored content; identify the commercial sponsor and avoid a publisher-style “from” identity that misleads. Include the advertiser's required unsubscribe/contact elements in email creative where the advertiser is the commercial sender/instigator.
- U.S. FTC truth-in-advertising principles apply; do not imply independent editorial endorsement where payment exists. Preserve the insertion order, approved creative, audience/geo, send date, and screenshots.
- For tracking pixels/click IDs, disclose analytics/personalisation in privacy notices and obtain consent where cookie/terminal-access rules require it. Avoid passing unnecessary recipient identifiers to sponsors; use aggregated reporting where possible.

### 4) Affiliate email traffic

- The affiliate, advertiser, network and publisher may each have roles. Contractually require lawful list acquisition, approved claims/creative, suppression synchronization, frequency caps, complaint handling, and audit logs. Prohibit affiliates from using scraped/purchased lists unless provenance meets target jurisdiction.
- U.S. FTC Endorsement Guides: affiliate relationships/material connections should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously so consumers can evaluate the recommendation. FTC's official Q&A gives “I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post” as an example and says disclosure should appear both in a video and near affiliate links where relevant. Advertisers can be liable for disseminating deceptive endorsements or failing to train/monitor paid endorsers.
- UK CAP Code: make commercial intent and paid/affiliate content obvious; use “Ad,” “Advertisement,” or similarly clear language in the communication, not buried terms. Claims must be substantiated and not omit material information.
- Email itself remains subject to CAN-SPAM / PECR / ePrivacy rules. A disclosure does **not** cure lack of consent or deceptive headers. Track which affiliate generated each send and retain the exact disclosure/creative version.

### 5) Direct mail

- **U.S.:** no CAN-SPAM overlay, but apply FTC/state deceptive-practices rules, sector advertising rules, privacy law, and contractual data-source limits. Use accurate sender identity, substantiated claims, clear offer terms, and a postal/online way to object. Screen against internal suppression/do-not-mail requests; do not repurchase suppressed people without a documented lawful reason.
- **UK/EU:** ICO says postal marketing is not covered by PECR, but GDPR/UK GDPR applies. Select and document a lawful basis (often legitimate interests with balancing test, or consent), provide transparent privacy information, honor objections to direct marketing without balancing them away, and retain only as long as needed. If using a broker, document source, recipients, purposes, data categories, retention and controller/processor arrangements.
- Include clear advertiser identity, offer limitations, response mechanism, and privacy notice URL/QR. Be cautious with profiling, sensitive data, children, health/financial offers, and geographic targeting; sector/national laws can add consent or disclosure requirements.

## Tracking, suppression, and privacy controls

- **Suppression:** central, durable suppression table keyed by normalized email/postal identity plus hashed variants where operationally needed; propagate to every agency, affiliate, publisher and broker; apply before each upload and send; keep an audit of opt-out time/source and propagation.
- **Consent evidence:** store wording/version, timestamp/time zone, source URL/form, affirmative action, sender/brand/category, channel, IP/device only if necessary, and proof of any later change/withdrawal. UK/EU consent must be freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous and as easy to withdraw as give (UK GDPR Art. 7; ICO guidance).
- **Privacy notices:** explain direct marketing purposes, lawful basis, recipients/categories (including brokers/publishers where relevant), source when data was not obtained directly, retention, rights and international transfers. For rented lists, do not rely solely on the broker's notice.
- **Tracking:** email opens are imperfect (caching, Apple Mail Privacy Protection, security scanners). Treat opens as measurement, not consent. Click IDs, pixels, UTM parameters, device IDs and postal matchback data can be personal data when linkable. Minimise fields, set retention, restrict access, and disclose analytics/personalisation. Cookie/terminal-access consent requirements may apply separately under ePrivacy/PECR.
- **Processor/security:** use contracts and security controls for ESPs, brokers, affiliate networks and list handlers; document deletion/return and subprocessors; assess international transfers (EU/UK adequacy/SCC/IDTA as applicable).
- **Children/sensitive sectors:** perform additional review for minors, health, finance, political, gambling, alcohol, supplements and other regulated offers. Do not use inferred sensitive data for targeting without specialist advice.

## Buyer red flags / reject list

- “100% opted-in” with no exact wording, date, source or sender/category named.
- Generic “partners” consent for a UK/EU consumer solo ad or rented list.
- Seller refuses sample consent/audit evidence or suppression synchronization.
- Publisher says advertiser is not responsible because “we send it.”
- Affiliate promises to “handle compliance” but will not disclose list source, domains, creative, or complaint data.
- No durable suppression process; re-uploading raw lists after unsubscribe.
- Sponsored newsletter/ad disguised as editorial or affiliate compensation hidden in terms/footer.
- Tracking that exports raw emails, IP/device data or purchase matching without purpose, notice, minimisation and retention controls.
