## Solo-ad marketplace/provider research

**Key distinction**

- **Marketplace:** connects buyers with multiple list owners; platform usually handles payment, delivery tracking, ratings, and disputes.
- **List owner / direct vendor:** owns or controls one or more email lists and sells placements directly; buyer must independently validate claims.
- **Traffic broker / managed network:** accepts the offer and click order, then selects an underlying list—or rotates across lists—for the buyer. You may not know which list actually mailed the offer.

## Practical buying mechanics

1. **Start with a test order**, typically a small click package rather than a large monthly commitment.
2. Require the vendor to state in writing:
   - whether the vendor owns the list or brokers traffic;
   - the exact niche, geography, and language;
   - whether clicks are unique and human-filtered;
   - minimum delivery volume and delivery window;
   - what happens to undelivered or invalid clicks;
   - refund/credit rules and the deadline for disputing delivery.
3. Use your own tracking:
   - dedicated landing page;
   - UTM parameters and a unique campaign ID;
   - first-party analytics;
   - server-side click logs where possible;
   - separate conversion tracking from vendor-reported clicks.
4. Ask for **recent anonymized campaign evidence**, not just testimonials:
   - delivered clicks versus claimed clicks;
   - unique visitors;
   - opt-in rate;
   - bounce/engagement indicators;
   - geography and timestamp distribution;
   - repeat exposure or duplicate-lead rate.
5. Treat “opt-in,” “permission-based,” “verified,” “fresh,” “real human,” and “clean traffic” as **unverified marketing claims unless backed by a consent explanation or audit trail**.
6. Avoid paying off-platform when a marketplace offers escrow, dispute handling, and platform-tracked delivery. For direct vendors, use a payment method with buyer protection and preserve the written terms.
7. Do not assume that a list subscriber’s consent to receive information from one publisher transfers automatically to a third-party advertiser. Confirm the disclosure, purpose, brand/category scope, and applicable email/ESP restrictions.

## Strongest and weakest options from the evidence

- **Best transaction-protection structure:** Udimi, because it is the only reviewed option clearly describing seller identity checks, platform-controlled payment, platform-controlled delivery, ratings, and dispute/refund handling. Its list-consent evidence still requires buyer diligence.
- **Most transparent low-cost mechanics:** 10DollarSoloAds and EasySoloAds publish concrete packages, targeting, and third-party tracking claims, but their refund terms become restrictive once campaigns start and they do not identify underlying list owners.
- **Highest risk:** TrafficForMe. Its sales page makes strong quality claims, while the live Trustpilot profile showed a 2.0/5 score, 11 reviews, no recent review-invitation history, and repeated complaints about delivery/support/refunds.
- **Useful for vendor discovery only:** SoloAdsX. Treat it as a directory and lead-generation channel, not as an escrowed marketplace.
- **Not a direct substitute for solo ads:** My Lead Gen Secret. It is closer to a permission-based lead/list distribution network and requires separate consent, exclusivity, and compliance verification.
