- **Completed:** Researched buyer due diligence and compliance for list rentals, solo ads, and affiliate email media buys using FTC, ICO, EUR-Lex/GDPR, California AG, Google, Yahoo, Mailchimp, and FTC endorsement guidance.
- **Key findings:**
  - Seller “opt-in” representations are insufficient; require cohort-level consent evidence, exact signup wording, source, timestamp, channel, frequency, advertiser/partner identity, and suppression history.
  - Under CAN-SPAM, both the promoted business and sending company may be liable; outsourcing delivery does not transfer responsibility. Commercial email requires truthful headers/subjects, postal address, clear opt-out, and opt-out handling within 10 business days.
  - UK PECR requires clear, specific, affirmative consent where applicable; pre-ticked boxes and generic third-party consent are inadequate. The instigator and contractor may both be responsible.
  - GDPR Article 7 requires demonstrable consent and easy withdrawal; Article 21 creates an unconditional direct-marketing objection right. CCPA/CPRA adds sale/share opt-outs and GPC handling, separate from email unsubscribe.
  - Google recommends spam rates below **0.10%** and avoiding **0.30%+**; Yahoo requires bulk senders to remain below **0.3%** and honor unsubscribes within **2 days**. There is no universal legal bounce threshold, so contracts should define source-level hard-bounce limits and pause/remediation rules.
  - Google and Yahoo specifically caution against purchased lists, unsolicited recipients, and affiliate spam. Mailchimp prohibits rented/purchased/third-party/co-registration/partner lists and requires consent evidence.
