## 1. List quality and relationship protection

`brian_kurtz/text/058-the-moose-on-the-table.txt`

- Kurtz says his early career was becoming “the world’s best list manager” when list selection was central to every campaign.
- Boardroom’s value was described as affluent executives, buyers, and subscribers who loved buying/subscribing through direct mail.
- The story of clearing an offer that was offensive to the list, despite a $100,000 check, demonstrates that list-owner judgment and relationship protection outrank a single rental fee.

**Repository implication:** a vendor directory must record audience relationship, offer-clearance standards, and risk—not just volume or price.

## 2. Research before mail

`brian_kurtz/text/043-the-politics-of-direct-marketing.txt`

- Kurtz describes Phillips Publishing as committed to list research and asking more questions before mailing than list brokers/managers wanted.
- He describes this as a defining reason Phillips was best-in-class.

**Repository implication:** the buyer checklist requires source, recency, selection history, usage restrictions, and test design before a buy.

## 3. Treat the list as people/family and segment thoroughly

`brian_kurtz/text/035-direct-marketers-saving-lives.txt`

- Kurtz attributes to Dick Benson the principle of looking at a list like family and segmenting thoroughly.

**Repository implication:** no “one giant list” recommendation. Every record should expose segment, source, behavior, recency, and test cell.

## 4. Product/list congruence and multi-channel thinking

`brian_kurtz/text/011-how-my-insomnia-led-to-over-200-million-in-sales.txt`

- The Boardroom/Bottom Line examples connect strong product selection, direct mail, books/newsletters, and later multi-channel opportunities.
- The Guthy-Renker example is used to show that a channel can become a broader opportunity rather than a one-off placement.

**Repository implication:** judge a media source by offer-channel fit and downstream value, not merely first-click economics.

## 5. Product development from the house file

`swipe_file_research_brian_kurtz/content/books_product.md`

- Kurtz describes using the Boardroom 7-million-name offline database and newsletter knowledge to develop products.
- He describes turning newsletter “greatest hits” into books and using customer knowledge to guide product selection.

**Repository implication:** the strongest long-term asset is first-party behavioral intelligence and a house-file feedback loop; rented media should improve that asset.

## 7. RFM and “lists are people”

`overdeliver_bonuses/extracted_text/the-lost-chapters-of-overdeliver-pdf.txt` lines 568–683:

- Recent buyers/inquirers are “hotlines”; recency often determines whether a list pays out.
- Frequency adds the multibuyer dimension; a frequent former customer may be more valuable than a recent one-time buyer.
- Monetary value completes RFM; tier customers by spend and combine it with recency/frequency.
- Kurtz explicitly describes lists as people and recommends surveying high-RFM customers to understand why and in what order they bought.

**Repository implication:** “list size” is a weak record. Strong records expose RFM or the nearest honest proxy and preserve a learning loop back into the house file.

## Historical names to treat carefully

Boardroom, Bottom Line/Personal, Phillips Publishing, Human Events, Healthy Directions, Meredith, Rodale, and related names are useful for understanding the tradition. Their historical significance should not be converted into a claim that a current rental or sponsorship product is available today.
