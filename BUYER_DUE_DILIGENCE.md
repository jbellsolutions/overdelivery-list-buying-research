# Buyer Due-Diligence Checklist

Use this before paying for a list rental, solo ad, email placement, newsletter sponsorship, affiliate email, or direct-mail list.

## A. Identify what is actually being sold

- [ ] Is this a named publisher/list owner, a broker, a marketplace, an affiliate network, a data vendor, or tracking software?
- [ ] Is the product a rented send, a sponsored placement, a dedicated email, a CPM/CPC/CPA buy, a physical address list, a data file, or a lead-generation service?
- [ ] Who sends the message? The owner, the platform, an affiliate, or us?
- [ ] Do we receive names/addresses, or only media exposure? If names are transferred, why is that lawful and permitted?
- [ ] Is the audience first-party, co-registration, third-party, scraped, modeled, or unknown?

## B. Ask for source intelligence

- [ ] Exact audience description and acquisition source.
- [ ] Date range and recency of acquisition.
- [ ] Buyer vs. subscriber vs. free registrant vs. clicker distinction.
- [ ] Categories purchased, products bought, and recency/frequency/value fields available.
- [ ] Geographic, demographic, device, and interest selects.
- [ ] Prior offer categories accepted or prohibited.
- [ ] Suppression and seed-list policy.
- [ ] Recent delivery, bounce, complaint, unsubscribe, and conversion reporting.
- [ ] At least one controlled test with a defined quantity and success criterion.

## C. Confirm permissions and compliance

- [ ] Written representation of the permission basis and send authority.
- [ ] Who is the legal sender and who processes opt-outs?
- [ ] CAN-SPAM, applicable state law, GDPR/UK PECR where relevant, and platform policies reviewed.
- [ ] Required physical address, identification, disclosures, and unsubscribe mechanism are handled.
- [ ] Suppression lists are honored and retained.
- [ ] Affiliate/creator disclosures are explicit where endorsements or commissions exist.
- [ ] No purchased email file is treated as permission to send without legal review.

## D. Protect the test

- [ ] Start with a small, pre-agreed test cell—not the full budget.
- [ ] Use a unique tracking URL, coupon, phone number, or promo code.
- [ ] Define attribution window before launch.
- [ ] Confirm whether clicks are unique, raw, bot-filtered, or guaranteed.
- [ ] Confirm whether opens are available and how they are measured; do not treat opens as sales intent.
- [ ] Use seed addresses and test rendering.
- [ ] Track complaints, refunds, chargebacks, leads, sales, contribution margin, and downstream opt-ins.
- [ ] Record creative version, list segment, date, placement, cost, and result in the media log.

## E. Protect the relationship

- [ ] Offer is congruent with the audience and publisher.
- [ ] Claims, advertorial language, and creative are acceptable to the owner.
- [ ] Complaint handling owner is named.
- [ ] No incentive or “bonus” can justify damaging a valuable audience relationship.
- [ ] If the first test works, negotiate a learning agenda: new selects, cadence, creative, and controlled rollouts.

## F. Red flags

- “Guaranteed sales” without a defined test and attribution rule.
- Large list size with no buyer/subscriber distinction.
- No owner or sender identity.
- Refusal to state how permission was obtained.
- No suppression or complaint process.
- Pressure to scale before the test is read.
- Testimonials without source-level metrics or independent context.
- A platform’s tracking capability being presented as proof of audience quality.
- A generic lead/data file being marketed as a permissioned newsletter audience.

## G. Contract-level stop rules

- [ ] Define hard-bounce, complaint, unsubscribe, duplicate-click, invalid-lead, and refund thresholds by source.
- [ ] Define pause/remediation rights if thresholds are exceeded; do not wait for the next billing cycle.
- [ ] Define make-good/refund rights for short delivery, invalid traffic, duplicate leads, or noncompliant sends.
- [ ] Name the party responsible for suppression updates and specify the service-level deadline.
- [ ] Preserve the exact signup language, consent source, timestamp, channel, geography, frequency, advertiser disclosure, and suppression history for any email or lead source.
- [ ] Obtain written offer-level approval for affiliate/email traffic; platform support for affiliate links is not universal email permission.
- [ ] Treat Google’s sub-0.10% spam recommendation, 0.30%+ danger zone, and Yahoo’s 0.3% bulk-sender ceiling as operational guardrails, not legal safe harbors.
- [ ] Confirm whether the ESP permits rented, purchased, third-party, co-registration, or partner lists. Some ESPs prohibit these categories even when the vendor claims opt-in.

## H. Required evidence packet

Before launch, save:

- [ ] current datacard/media kit or offer page;
- [ ] terms, privacy, refund, and traffic-policy pages;
- [ ] written clearance/approval email;
- [ ] consent/provenance explanation;
- [ ] suppression and unsubscribe procedure;
- [ ] tracking specification and attribution window;
- [ ] test order, invoice, and delivery report;
- [ ] final creative and landing page version.
