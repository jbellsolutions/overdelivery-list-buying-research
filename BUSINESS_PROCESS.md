# Overdeliver — Business Process

**Operating standard:** Buy access, not databases. Test placements, not promises. Capture the response. Build the house file.

**Framework:** Brian Kurtz / *Over Delivery* direct-response discipline.

This is the practical operating layer for the research corpus. Use it to decide **which category to use, what evidence to require, how to run the first test, when to stop, and when to scale**.

---

## 1. Process map

| Stage | Decision | Required output | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Strategy brief | What are we selling, to whom, and why now? | One-page campaign brief | Offer, margin, audience, and success event are defined |
| 2. Category selection | Which access lane matches the objective? | Category selection record | The lane sells the access we actually need |
| 3. Source universe | Which publishers, brokers, platforms, and owners are candidates? | Shortlist with source URLs | Every candidate has an official evidence trail |
| 4. Vendor qualification | Is the source real, relevant, reachable, and testable? | Vendor scorecard | Evidence meets the minimum threshold |
| 5. Compliance clearance | Can this audience, sender, offer, and jurisdiction be used? | Permission/compliance packet | Consent, roles, suppression, and claims are documented |
| 6. Test design | What is the smallest useful test? | Test card and tracking plan | Budget, holdout, attribution, and stop rules are fixed |
| 7. Launch control | Can operations execute without losing source or response data? | Approved creative, QA record, suppression snapshot | All launch checks pass |
| 8. Measurement | Did the source produce qualified economics and a clean experience? | Results card | Revenue, contribution, complaints, refunds, and lead quality are known |
| 9. Scale or stop | Should this lane receive more budget, a refinement, or a kill? | Decision record | Decision follows evidence, not enthusiasm |
| 10. Owned-audience compounding | What learning and permission become house assets? | Segments, follow-up, source history | Every usable lead/customer is routed into a lawful first-party system |

**No stage may be skipped because a seller is familiar, a platform is large, or a page says “opt-in.”**

## 2. Choose the correct category

### A. Publisher and newsletter media

**Use when:** the offer has strong audience congruence and the goal is trusted reach, qualified traffic, or first-party lead capture.

**What is purchased:** sponsorship, native placement, dedicated send, newsletter insertion, or publisher media inventory.

**Do not confuse it with:** a transferable subscriber file or permission to upload addresses into your ESP.

**Minimum evidence:** audience description, geography, paid/free mix, placement, sender identity, recent delivery and complaint data, reporting definition, make-good terms, offer/creative clearance.

**Primary test:** one publisher, one placement, one landing page, one tracking ID, fixed budget.

### B. Solo ads and dedicated sends

**Use when:** a small, direct-response test can reveal whether a specific publisher's audience responds to a specific offer.

**What is purchased:** the publisher sends approved creative to its audience.

**Minimum evidence:** source and consent basis, actual sender, suppression process, seed test, delivery evidence, refund/replace terms, complaint and bounce reporting.

**Rule:** clicks are not proof of permission or quality. Never scale on clicks alone.

### C. Affiliate and performance partnerships

**Use when:** the offer can support CPA, revenue share, referral, creator, or publisher economics and the advertiser can control claims and customer experience.

**What is purchased:** partner distribution plus tracking and payout mechanics.

**Minimum evidence:** approved promotion method, partner/source identity, creative and claim approval, disclosure, frequency controls, suppression, fraud/refund handling, attribution window.

**Rule:** network tracking does not automatically authorize email promotion. Get offer-level approval in writing.

### D. Postal list rental and direct mail

**Use when:** the audience has buyer/subscriber behavior and the campaign benefits from physical mail, modeled selects, or RFM targeting.

**What is purchased:** named-list access, select criteria, postal deployment, or broker/manager service.

**Minimum evidence:** list owner, universe definition, recency/frequency/monetary selects, merge/purge, usage rights, suppression, matchback method, timing, and minimums.

**Rule:** postal is not email permission. Keep a separate channel, consent, and suppression analysis.

### E. Data, modeling, enrichment, and hygiene

**Use when:** the objective is direct mail, modeling, append, segmentation, address hygiene, or lawful deployment—not shortcut email acquisition.

**What is purchased:** records, modeled attributes, enrichment, hygiene, or deployment service.

**Minimum evidence:** collection source, permitted use, data roles, fields, recency, match rate, suppression, retention/deletion, transfer restrictions, and contract terms.

**Rule:** a data vendor's ability to provide a record does not prove permission to market to it.

### F. Discovery databases, associations, and relationship channels

**Use when:** the team needs current owners, publishers, brokers, media contacts, conferences, or market intelligence.

**What is purchased:** discovery and relationship infrastructure—not audience access itself.

**Minimum evidence:** current contact path, official source, role clarity, and a plan to validate the actual media/product offering.

**Rule:** discovery records become candidates; they do not become approved sources until diligence is complete.

### G. Tracking and compliance infrastructure

**Use when:** the campaign needs source IDs, attribution, suppression, lead validation, consent evidence, or partner controls.

**What is purchased:** measurement and operational control—not audience inventory.

**Minimum evidence:** data map, event definitions, retention, access controls, suppression sync, and export/reconciliation plan.

## 3. The operating packet

Create one packet per source/campaign. Store it with the campaign ID and link the relevant repository evidence.

### 3.1 Campaign brief

- Campaign ID and owner
- Product, offer, price, gross margin, refund/chargeback exposure
- Primary audience and excluded audience
- Desired action: lead, sale, booked call, application, referral, or subscriber
- Geographic and jurisdiction scope
- Maximum test budget
- Target contribution margin or allowable acquisition cost
- Follow-up sequence and first-party capture method

### 3.2 Source scorecard

Score each candidate 0–3:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Audience truth | Unknown | Broad claim | Some evidence | Cohort/source evidence |
| Congruence | Weak | Plausible | Strong | Proven buyer behavior |
| Permission/role clarity | Unknown | Vague | Documented | Auditable and channel-specific |
| Testability | No test path | Seller-defined only | Trackable | Controlled and repeatable |
| Reporting | None | Aggregate clicks | Delivery/conversion | Raw events/cohort detail |
| Operational fit | Unclear | Manual risk | Workable | Suppression/QA integrated |
| Relationship quality | Unknown | Transactional | Responsive | Protects audience and improves |

**Approval rule:** no launch with a score below 2 on permission/role clarity, testability, or reporting. A high total score cannot override a failed compliance gate.

### 3.3 Compliance and clearance packet

Require before launch:

- Exact audience source and collection path
- Consent or lawful-basis explanation for the channel and jurisdiction
- Form/privacy-notice version and collection dates where applicable
- Advertiser, sender, publisher, broker, ESP, affiliate, and processor roles
- Suppression intake, propagation, and SLA
- Approved offer, claims, creative, disclosure, sender identity, and landing page
- Data-use, retention, deletion, and onward-transfer limits
- Incident, complaint, refund, invalid-lead, and pause procedures
- Contract owner and evidence location

**Hard stop:** vague “100% opt-in,” generic “partners,” scraped/harvested data, no suppression path, disguised sponsored content, or missing offer approval.

### 3.4 Test card

- Source and category
- Audience/cohort and geography
- Placement or delivery type
- Creative/version and landing page
- Budget and volume cap
- Tracking ID, promo code, phone number, or source field
- Seed/test procedure
- Attribution window and primary success event
- Secondary quality events: complaints, bounces, unsubscribes, refunds, chargebacks, invalid leads
- Pause thresholds and make-good/refund terms
- Decision date and owner

### 3.5 Results card

Record:

- Sent, delivered, bounces, complaints, unsubscribes, clicks
- Leads, qualified leads, sales, revenue, refunds, chargebacks
- Contribution margin and effective acquisition cost
- Conversion and quality by source/cohort/partner
- Customer-service or brand issues
- What was learned about audience, offer, creative, and follow-up
- Next decision: scale, refine, retest, hold, or stop

## 4. Launch checklist

### Before approval

- [ ] Category and objective match.
- [ ] Source is linked to an official page or media kit.
- [ ] Audience, sender, and product being purchased are explicit.
- [ ] Consent/provenance and jurisdiction are documented.
- [ ] Global suppression is checked and synchronized.
- [ ] Offer, claims, disclosures, From identity, and landing page are approved.
- [ ] Tracking and attribution are tested.
- [ ] Budget, volume cap, contribution threshold, and stop rules are written.
- [ ] Refund, make-good, invalid-lead, and incident terms are written.

### During launch

- [ ] Seed/test segment reviewed.
- [ ] Delivery and authentication checked.
- [ ] Complaint, bounce, unsubscribe, and deferral signals monitored.
- [ ] Source/cohort IDs preserved.
- [ ] Abnormal behavior triggers an immediate pause.

### After launch

- [ ] Raw delivery and conversion data exported.
- [ ] Suppressions and invalids propagated.
- [ ] Refunds, chargebacks, complaints, and customer feedback reconciled.
- [ ] Results card completed.
- [ ] Usable leads/customers routed into the lawful owned-audience system.
- [ ] Vendor score and source history updated.

## 5. Stop, refine, or scale rules

### Stop immediately when

- Consent/provenance cannot be produced.
- Sender, advertiser, or suppression roles are unclear.
- Complaints, bounces, invalids, fraud, or refunds materially exceed the agreed baseline.
- Creative or claims change without approval.
- The source cannot provide the agreed reporting.
- The campaign damages sender reputation, publisher trust, or customer experience.

### Refine when

- The source is congruent but the offer, creative, landing page, follow-up, or segment is weak.
- Traffic is real but lead quality or contribution is below target.
- One cohort performs materially better than the aggregate.

### Scale when

- The source passes the compliance and operational gates.
- The result meets contribution and quality thresholds across a repeatable test.
- Cohort-level performance is understood.
- The owner can preserve suppression, reporting, and relationship quality at higher volume.
- The next increment has a defined budget and a new learning objective.

**Scale is a controlled repetition of a proven mechanism, not a larger blind purchase.**

## 7. Management cadence

- **Weekly:** review active tests, complaints, bounces, invalids, refunds, and source-level economics.
- **Per campaign:** complete the packet before launch and the results card after the measurement window.
- **Monthly:** rank categories and sources by contribution, quality, learning value, and relationship health.
- **Quarterly:** retire stale records, recheck media kits/terms, refresh suppression logic, and move proven sources into an approved-source list.

This process is an operating guide, not legal advice. Confirm current law, platform policy, consent, sender responsibility, and offer clearance before deployment.
